# Debt-to-Income (DTI) Ratio Policy

## Overview
Debt-to-income ratio (DTI) is the percentage of a borrower's gross monthly income that
goes toward monthly debt payments, including the proposed mortgage payment.

## Section 1: Standard Thresholds
- Conventional loans: maximum DTI of 45%, up to 50% with compensating factors
  (reserves >= 6 months, credit score >= 720, or a loan-to-value ratio below 75%).
- FHA loans: maximum DTI of 43%, up to 50% with documented compensating factors.
- VA loans: no hard DTI ceiling, but DTI above 41% requires additional residual-income
  analysis.
- USDA loans: maximum DTI of 41%, front-end ratio capped at 29%.

## Section 2: High-Risk Classification
Any application with a DTI at or above 43% is flagged internally as **high-risk** for
portfolio-monitoring purposes, regardless of loan type, and is routed to a senior
underwriter for secondary review.

## Section 3: Documentation Requirements
Underwriters must recompute DTI using verified income (paystubs, W-2s, or tax returns for
self-employed borrowers) rather than the applicant-reported figure before final approval.
